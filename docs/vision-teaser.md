## THE TEASER

Somewhere in the Eifel mountains there is a road through the trees where, on certain
mornings, anyone with a car and a dream can drive as fast as they dare. The locals call
it the Green Hell. The licenses call it Touristenfahrten. You will call it *the commute*.

You start with nothing but a licence you had to earn — a real one: hill starts on a ramp
with a clutch that bites, parallel parking a examiner would fail you for, an emergency
stop inside a painted box. The world needs drivers. Somebody has to move the crates,
deliver the parts, carry the passengers, get the kittens to the vet. The economy runs on
wheels, and every wheel has a person behind it — or something learning to be one.

You take jobs. You save. You make mistakes — the car is honest about them. Feed it bad
fuel and it runs rough. Lock the fronts and the flat spots are on the tyres, in the marks
they leave, in the lap times they cost you. The engine is a real thing: it idles, it
warms up, it stalls if you dump a clutch you didn't deserve to dump, and you restart it
with the key like a person, in the rain, while the traffic learns to go around you.

Because the traffic isn't scripted. The other drivers *watch you* — your telemetry is
their textbook — and they watch themselves, every run recorded, every mistake remembered.
They take their exams. Some fail and try again. The good ones get hired; the best ones
open shops, blend brake compounds in the back room, and sell you pads whose promises you
can check by driving, because in this world the physics doesn't negotiate.

Somewhere along the way you buy your first Porsche. It isn't given to you and it isn't
about money — chief test drivers earn the right, the way the old Factory used to. You
drive it to the Ring on roads that come from the map of the real Earth: out of Cluj,
over the Carpathians where the suspension tells you every truth about every bump, down
to the coast, across the continent — fuel stops included, the cat riding shotgun,
complaining, in a world you could almost photograph but can't quite, because it's a
cartoon — a warm, hand-made, slightly-too-colorful cartoon — drawn over a simulation
that does not know how to lie.

One weekend — the dream, the one we're building toward — at least one human driver and
the AI drivers of this world will all be on the same Green Hell at the same time. Nobody
will be able to tell, from the driving alone, which is which. And the humans, watching,
will not be able to tell either — because the machines learned it from us.

**Real sim. Cartoon world. The 0.0.0 of Matrix.**

## THE STORY — how you live in it

You are one of the world's test drivers. Not the hero; a *professional*, with a rank,
a reputation, and a cat.

- **Your profile is your life**: your licence (L0 citizen, L1 factory entry, and up the
  rank ladder — test driver, race driver, chief test driver), your jobs, your exams,
  your cars, your garage in your home town. You are the only person you know who can
  drive like that — and in this world, so is everyone else who can.
- **The Factory** is the old heart of the world: a proving ground where the craft is
  kept. Its test pad taught you; its ladder ranks you; its chief test drivers get the
  right to own a Porsche. The Factory's exams are honest: the clock starts when you
  cross the line, not when you're ready.
- **The world outside is connected** — no walls. Cities where people live, houses along
  the roads, the Alps with their passes, Monte Carlo with its harbor ring, Corsica with
  its coast, and the Eifel with the Green Hell. You drive from one to another, because
  the roads are the point. Sometimes the streets are empty and it's just you and the
  machine. Sometimes you're stuck behind a Lada carrying hay, and it's a blessing,
  because the cat is asleep on the parcel shelf and waking her is not an option.
- **The cat chose you.** That's the rule of cats here: she consented. You stopped,
  you parked so the traffic protected her, you waited until she liked you, and she
  got in. She has nine lives, a food budget in your economy, and an opinion of your
  driving. Scare her too often and the tail puffs; scare her once too often and
  she's gone — out the door and down the street, and you will see her again in a
  week, walking the other way, with someone else. *Drive like someone is trusting
  you. Someone is.*

## THE MACHINE — what's real under the hood

Everything above rides on a simulation that is already running, already certified:

- **A drivetrain of honest states**: an engine with inertia, torque curve, friction,
  idle and a fuel-cut limiter that bounces; a clutch that slips, bites, launches and
  stalls if mismanaged (and a starter, because that's what happens then); a gearbox
  with reflected inertia; axle wheel speeds whose slip is measured, not faked.
- **Tyres with a temperature-aware future and a progressive breakaway present**; a
  suspension of four real spring/damper corners with anti-roll bars, over a road
  profile you can *feel* — bumps measured in millimetres and hills in metres.
- **A 900-degree steering wheel** turned by a hand that takes a third of a second,
  through a 16.4:1 rack, because a road car is not a kart.
- **An economy that cannot lie**: materials are parameter sets on the physics
  (carbon, metallic, organic, ceramic — each honest at temperature), parts are
  recipes, shops are vendors of recipes, and every claim is testable by driving.
- **A build pipeline instead of walls**: the roads come from open data (OSM, LiDAR)
  baked on your machine into original, hand-imperfect, streamed-by-manifest regions.
  The world is inspired by the map of the Earth; the geometry is ours.

501→552 headless checks and counting. Byte-identical test runs, because a simulation
that isn't deterministic isn't a simulation.

## THE STAIRCASE — what is already real, and what comes next

**Under your wheels tonight** (verified, certified, in your repo):
force-based chassis; true drivetrain; real suspension; the 900° wheel; missions that
end somewhere; medals — *gold on all five* is already on the leaderboard, first try.
Telemetry is being built as you read this: every run recorded, best times remembered.

**The next flights of stairs** (each already designed):
driver control profiles and pedals that feel like feet; fuel, exhaust, oil temperature,
materials; toggles for the electronics (turn the TC off and meet the tyres for real);
tyre marks on the tarmac; the licence ladder with its ramp and its examiner; the
garage; the shop; the town; the traffic that learns; the connected countries; the cat.

**And then the weekend.** One weekend: at least one human, on the Green Hell, among
the AI drivers this world raised on recordings of us — a timed tourist session,
silent except for engines, nobody able to say for sure which of the cars in the
mirror is a person.

That's the dream. It's on the roadmap in pieces, each one load-bearing, nothing
throwaway: **real sim, cartoon world.** The shop doesn't sleep. The driver does.
